The prompt

Every decision sends the same parts in the same order:

  1. Rules and strategy (about 6,400 characters): the rules of backgammon, strategy principles (blots and points, distribution, exposure, blocking, hitting, anchors), the best opening plays from computer rollouts, and the match rules above. A longer version with a glossary, game plans and a worked example goes to chat models; decision models get this short one because of Open-Jev's 4,096-token limit.
  2. Score and time: match score, round and game, turn number, both clocks.
  3. Position from the mover's side: checkers on every point, bar, borne off, pip counts.
  4. Race: the pip lead and whether the two sides are still in contact.
  5. The last 10 moves of this game, both sides, in the mover's numbering (league #13 on).
  6. Dice, then the question: Which of the following moves makes a point in front of the opponent's checkers, or reduces your single pieces, or gets you closer to your home?
  7. The legal plays, each followed by what the position looks like after it, computed by the program.

A real request from league #13, Jev 1.13.0 to move (the rules part is left out):

Score: you 1, opponent 1 (tied).
Match: round 2 of 3, game 1 of 2 in this round.
Turn 24 of at most 300 in this game.
Clock: 0:30 free delay for this decision, then your bank: 5:00 of 5:00 left in this game (opponent 5:00). …

Position (your numbering; you move 24 -> 1):
  your checkers:     24:1 20:1 15:1 4:1 3:4 2:2 1:5
  opponent checkers: 11:1 12:2 17:2 18:2 19:5 23:3
  on the bar: you 0, opponent 0
  borne off:  you 0, opponent 0
  pip count:  you 84, opponent 106
Race: you lead by 22 pips; the checkers are still in contact.
Last 10 moves of this game (oldest first, your numbering):
  turn 14, you rolled 2-5: bar/23 21/16
  turn 15, opponent rolled 3-3: 12/15 12/15 15/18 15/18
  …
  turn 22, you rolled 4-6: bar/21 21/15
  turn 23, opponent rolled 4-5: 19/23 6/11
Dice: 2-5

Which of the following moves makes a point in front of the opponent's checkers, or reduces your single pieces, or gets you closer to your home?

Options (8):
24/22 20/15: hits 0; makes the 15 point; leaves 2 blots, the opponent hits with 31 of 36 rolls; you hold 4 points (3 in your home board), longest prime 3; pips: you 77, opponent 106
4/2 20/15: hits 0; makes the 15 point; leaves 1 blot, the opponent hits with 36 of 36 rolls; you hold 4 points (3 in your home board), longest prime 3; pips: you 77, opponent 106
15/13 13/8: hits 0; makes no new point; leaves 4 blots, the opponent hits with 36 of 36 rolls; …
… 5 more

Answer: 4/2 20/15 (probability 0.41; 24/22 20/15 had 0.39)

The numbers after each play are exact. The opponent's hitting rolls are counted by generating its legal plays for each of the 36 rolls and checking whether any of them hits. In the example the model narrowly preferred the play with one blot that every roll hits over the one with two blots that 31 rolls hit.
