## Late entries (League 1 table, required pairs = 17; GPT-6 Luna and Mercury Decide not required, Ümit #5220)

Method per arm:
- **Route.** Every decision turn and every exchange was tallied: provider, quantization, prompt version, harness commit and dirty flag; the request model, provider pin, reasoning and template keys, max_tokens, temperature and logprobs; the reply's model echo, provider and fingerprint, finish reason and error.
- **Legality.** Every move was recomputed with `tools/v3/rules.py` (`legal_plays`), against `legal_count` and `move = legal[chosen_index]`.
- **Indexing.** `option_order` must be a permutation, `option_order[chosen_position] = chosen_index`, and the letter must match.
- **Answer parsing.** Every request's option list was parsed (chat arms) or its criteria read (decision services). The answer was re-read from the stored reply: the letter-token probabilities summed and normalised, or the JSON choice, or the service's choice. Then:
  - the final answer must equal the move;
  - the stored probability vector length must equal the options;
  - the non-null probabilities must equal the final request's options and values;
  - split decisions: group size, every play covered once, each group winner in the next round.
- **Games.** Every game's position chain was replayed turn by turn, mirrored legs' dice compared, and turn numbering checked for gaps. Duplicate games and turns, and aborted, failed or voided matches, were checked too.

## Learning lanes ("Did they improve?") and Muse Ember's 200-position set

Method (applied identically to every lane arm):
1. **Settings and route.** For every stored test answer (`answers`) and every model game turn (`turns`, player 1, decided_by model/fallback), I tallied spec, provider, quantization, reasoning, prompt_version, order_seed, harness_commit and harness_dirty. For every raw exchange (`answer_exchanges`, `exchanges`, lesson `call.json`), I recorded the model echo (`/model`, or Antigravity `/response/modelVersion`), the provider echo, the finish/stop reason, and any error object. I also built a request-settings signature: the body with the prompt text and the enum list removed. Each arm's signature was compared with its `v3.db players.settings` and with the plan's §4 table. The E1/E2/E3 prompts were compared by hash per position.
2. **I/O.** Every decision was replayed with `tools/v3/rules.py` (`from_json` + `legal_plays`) for games. Tests used the stored `test_positions.jsonl` legal list. Checks:
   - `legal_count` matches the legal list, and the stored move is `legal[chosen_index]`.
   - `option_order` is a permutation, and it equals the test set's order.
   - `option_order[chosen_position] == chosen_index`.
   - The letter equals `A+chosen_position` (null at position ≥26 by design).
   - The probability vector length equals the options shown. Logprob sums are within 0.02 of 1.
   - For group splits: `group_size` matches the split rule, and the non-null probabilities equal the final round's winners.
   - Game turns match `plays` (n, chosen).
3. **Parser to scorer.** improve.py `metrics_from` counts a row with `error` as failed (re-asked until 200/200). A row with `invalid` is left out of the shares and the PR. In games, `decided_by=fallback` means a random legal play (runner.rs/players `decide_held`). Every invalid or fallback row's deciding exchange was classified:
   - **(a)** no model content: an error payload, a choice-level `error`, `finish_reason=error`, no candidates, or no response;
   - **(b)** a real model reply with no option letter.

   I recomputed every aggregate's T10/E metrics with improve.py's own `metrics_from`; all match the stored aggregates.
4. **Completeness.** Matches, games and series attempts were checked against the archive (`attempt-N`), along with `match.done`, the seeds (`series_seed`), duplicates (unique keys plus distinct position_id per test), and in_flight (games 1, tests ≤4).
5. **Lessons.** Per series: the count of lesson files and call.json, an empty-or-errored reply check, and `call.text` = the response's content (hash compare). `lessons-gNN.txt` = `limit_lessons(call.text)` (≤5 lessons, ≤150 words). Every game turn's system prompt ends with `contexts/after-g(N-1)`, and game 1 has no notes. Every T10 request ends with `after-g10`. E runs carry no notes. Lesson call times fall inside the final attempt's game windows.

### D1: DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (on): provider errors scored as invalid model answers
- **Evidence.** 7 responses carry an OpenRouter choice-level `error` with `code 502` and `finish_reason: "error"`, with no content (only partial reasoning, 240–19,958 tokens). The runner parsed them as replies with no letter, so they were stored as `invalid=1` (tests) and `decided_by=fallback` (game). They were never marked failed, so they were never re-asked.
  - Tests: answer ids **204, 205** (`s01 | a1 | T10`, positions lp-198455, lp-26641, exchanges 281, 283) and **1465, 1466, 1491, 1492** (`s05 | a1 | T10`, lp-31646, lp-173367, lp-90458, lp-18007; exchanges 2017, 2018, 2054, 2057).
  - Game: turn **19** (match 1 = S1, game 1, turn_no 19, 112 options; exchange 10). A random legal play was made, and GNUbg scored it at a loss of 1.149.
- **Affected scope.**
  - S1-T10: answered 200, but invalid 5 → really 3 invalid, 2 failed.
  - S5-T10: invalid 7 → really 3 invalid, 4 failed.
  - Both tests miss the plan's §7 gate ("200 of 200 … no failed request left"). The answer rate and the invalid counts are overstated. The shares are unaffected, because invalid rows are left out.
  - S1 game 1 continued from a random play caused by a transport error. That changes the rest of the game, its result, and the S1 lesson record. The invalid turn itself is excluded from the record.
- **Root cause.** `players/llm.rs` (`answer_decision` / `parse_chat_completion` path) treats a 200 response whose choice carries `error` / `finish_reason=error` as a model reply. It should be a failed request that is retried.
