Changes during the leagues

  • Voided matches. The first matches of LFM2.5-24B-A2B (off, on) and Qwen3.8 Flash (off) were voided on 19 October and replay from scratch with the same dice. The runner had read the letter probabilities from the wrong token: for LFM2.5, Liquid's API returns probabilities for the reasoning tokens too, so every decision read a letter from the reasoning; for Qwen3.8 Flash, some decisions read the "c" of a split "choice" key. It now reads them at the reply's letter and counts a mismatch as invalid. LFM2.5 now runs on our GPU from Liquid's bf16 checkpoint, with the letter read after a prefilled "Answer:".
  • Qwen3.8-27B (off) replayed. On our vLLM server the list of token probabilities ends with an end-of-message token after the letter, so the runner found no letter and played the letter Qwen had sampled instead of its most likely one, in every one of its decisions there. The runner now skips such trailing tokens; Qwen3.8-27B (off)'s affected matches were voided on 20 October and replayed with the same dice. No other setup was affected.
  • Withdrawn. Qwen3.8 Flash Next (off, on): with reasoning on it took many minutes per decision on a DGX Spark. Its matches are removed from the league. Qwen3.8 Flash (off), through OpenRouter, joined instead.
  • Moved. On 19 October League 1 became decision models only: Gemma 4 31B (off, on) and Qwen3.8-27B (off, on) moved to League 2, and their League 1 matches no longer count. GPT-6 Luna's first entry, through the ChatGPT subscription as a chat model, was removed; it plays through OpenAI's Decisions API. The other new decision models joined League 1 the same day; Fastino GLiDE and Decider v1.2 27B joined on 20 October.
  • Hardware. Clef, Cygnet and Decider v1.1 27B moved from our RTX PRO 6000 cards to DGX Spark (GB10) machines on 16 October, with the same checkpoints and settings (replayed requests: the same play in all). Open-Jev stayed, because on a DGX Spark it sometimes chose another play. Gemma 4 31B ran on a DGX Spark from 19 October 18:36 to 22:26 UTC, then on an RTX PRO 6000 again (the same play in all replayed requests). Qwen3.8-27B started on Cerebras through OpenRouter, where it finished no match, and then moved to our GPU in fp16 (the same top letter in most replayed requests). From 20 October 21:10 UTC Gemma 4 31B, LFM2.5-24B-A2B and Qwen3.8-27B played on DGX Sparks with the same checkpoints and settings (the same top letter in all replayed requests), so that DeepSeek V4.1 Flash could have all of our RTX PRO 6000 cards; on 21 October 01:25-05:00 UTC they played their last non-DeepSeek matches on the RTX PRO 6000 cards again (LFM2.5 is not deterministic, so its replays agree less often).
  • DeepSeek V4.1 Flash on our GPUs. From 20 October 21:56 UTC, DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (off, on) plays on our own SGLang server instead of Fireworks through OpenRouter: the 17 October revision of the published weights (FP4 routed experts, FP8/BF16 elsewhere); Fireworks does not say which revision it serves. On stored Fireworks requests our server usually chose the same top letter, but not always; it is not deterministic, and Fireworks replaying its own stored answers did not always agree either.
  • Gemini by subscription. With no Gemini API key, Gemini 3.8 Flash (low, high) plays through the Google Antigravity subscription from 19 October 12:46 UTC: the same model, prompt, thinking levels and constrained letter. On the smoke positions it nearly always chose the same play as the API.
  • Paused matches. A match that fails on a provider error (OpenRouter credit or rate limits, a server restart) waits and resumes later from its last stored turn; the other matches go on.

About the anchor

The anchor, GNU Backgammon 1.08 at its Beginner level (0-ply, deterministic noise 0.06), ran on one x86-64 build only, and on x86-64 it is not the Beginner that GNU Backgammon intends. Its deterministic-noise code sums the hash bytes as char, which is signed on x86-64, but subtracts the mean of unsigned bytes, so every noise draw is shifted by about −7 standard deviations. The anchor understates its own winning chances and plays for gammons.

  • Another build plays differently. On the 1,000 v1 positions, the x86-64 and ARM builds of the same package choose different plays in 659; with the noise off, in 6, all exact ties. Error rate 21.8 on x86-64 against 16.8 on ARM.
  • Gammons in self-play. 72% of games end in a gammon or backgammon on x86-64, 40% on ARM and about 30% without noise.

So v3's anchor is "GNUbg 1.08 Beginner, x86-64 build", and its results are reproducible on x86-64 only: a replay there gives the recorded plays. Later releases of the benchmark use a corrected build.
